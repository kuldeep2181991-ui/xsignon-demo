// Answers For You

Common Questions About Cybersecurity Solutions.

Talk to a Security Expert
  • How do SMBs implement Zero Trust architecture?
    Implementing Zero Trust security for small and mid-size businesses typically follows four stages: (1) Identity Assessment - cataloging all user, device, and service identities and their current access rights; (2) Least Privilege Enforcement - ensuring every identity has only the minimum access required; (3) Continuous Verification - deploying FIDO2-compliant passwordless authentication so access is verified at every session; and (4) Monitoring & Governance - continuously auditing access patterns and automating policy enforcement. NIST Special Publication 800-207 provides the technical reference for Zero Trust architecture design.
  • What is shadow AI risk management?
    Shadow AI refers to the unauthorized use of AI tools - generative AI assistants, code platforms, third-party APIs - by employees without IT awareness. It creates data exposure risk, access sprawl (AI agents accumulating unreviewed access rights), and compliance violations under HIPAA, GDPR, or SOC 2. Shadow AI risk management involves discovering unsanctioned AI tool usage, governing AI identities with the same controls applied to human users, and enforcing policy aligned with the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI-RMF).
  • What is data security posture management (DSPM)?
    Data Security Posture Management (DSPM) provides continuous visibility into where sensitive data resides, who has access, how it is being used, and what controls protect it - across cloud, hybrid, and on-premises environments. Unlike reactive DLP tools, DSPM continuously assesses data asset security and flags risks before a breach occurs. XSignOn's Posture One product incorporates DSPM capabilities within a broader identity and access governance framework.
  • Why do businesses need post-quantum cryptography now?
    Current widely-used encryption standards — RSA and ECC — can theoretically be broken by quantum computers. In August 2024, NIST finalized the first three post-quantum cryptography standards: FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA), and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA). The urgency is driven by "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks where adversaries collect encrypted data today to decrypt once quantum computing matures. XSignOn's quantum-safe security solutions help businesses conduct a cryptographic inventory and execute a phased NIST-compliant migration.
  • How does FIDO2 passwordless authentication
    work for enterprises?
    FIDO2 is an open standard from the FIDO Alliance and W3C that enables passwordless authentication using public key cryptography. A key pair is bound to the user's device — the private key never leaves it, and no password is transmitted. This makes it structurally resistant to phishing, credential stuffing, and man-in-the-middle attacks. Enterprise FIDO2 deployment integrates with Active Directory, Azure AD, and Okta, and can enforce biometric verification at the device level.
  • What does continuous compliance automation cover for
    SOC 2, HIPAA, and GDPR?
    Continuous compliance automation maintains and documents adherence to regulatory frameworks on an ongoing basis. The platform automatically collects evidence of control effectiveness, maps it to specific requirements, and generates audit-ready reports on demand. For SOC 2, it maintains a defensible evidence trail for all Trust Service Criteria. For HIPAA, it documents Security Rule compliance. For GDPR, it supports Articles 5, 25, and 32 requirements through automated technical controls and documented policies.
  • What is the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI-RMF)?
    The NIST AI-RMF, published in January 2023, is a voluntary US federal framework for identifying, assessing, and managing AI system risks. It is organized around four functions: Govern (AI risk governance structures), Map (identifying AI risks in context), Measure (analyzing evaluated risks), and Manage (prioritizing and addressing risks ongoing). XSignOn's AI & Digital Governance service implements NIST AI-RMF-aligned controls with particular focus on AI identity and access risk management.
